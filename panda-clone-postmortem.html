What we were trying to do

The expert is good and unusable. It solves a quadratic program at every control step, selecting 300 columns from a bank of roughly 180,000, and takes 3.2 seconds to do it against a 20 ms control period. The clone exists to make that policy run in a forward pass, exactly as it did on the unicycle and the 2-DoF reacher.

The clone reads a 99-dimensional window — the last five applied targets, the last five outputs, the current output, the goal offset, and a buffer-validity flag — and must emit the expert's commanded joint target q_des.

Where the difficulty comes from

Seven attempts

In order. Each one targets a mechanism we had measured first.

  1. Baseline behavioral cloning

    The pipeline as ported from the reacher: 100 expert-driven episodes, 150 steps each, 15,000 labelled rows, one-step regression on absolute q_des.

    reach 2/78 failed

  2. Reparameterize the output

    The learnable signal is the 0.02 rad increment, which is 2% of the target's standard deviation — so a mean-squared error on the absolute target barely registers it. Tried absolute, then delta from current joints, then increment from the previous target.

    reach 2 → 7 → 9/78, directional accuracy 0.70 → 0.87 real, insufficient

  3. DAgger, one round

    Cloning trains on the expert's states; the deployed clone visits its own. Collected 100 clone-driven episodes with the expert labelling every state it reached.

    reach 2 → 2 no effect

    A student at 3% reach wanders where the teacher is unreliable: only 36% of clone-visited states fall inside the expert's validity radius, against 57% of expert-visited ones.

  4. Spread the data further

    200 placements left the nearest training state 4.36 away in standardized units, with only 1% of states having a neighbour close enough for the expert's decision to transfer. Re-spent the identical solver budget as 400 placements × 35 steps instead of 100 × 150.

    coverage +16% (5.51 → 4.64), reach 8 → 5 no effect

  5. Fix the training

    Training accuracy 0.87 against held-out 0.37 is overfitting, and the validation split was by row — with 150 correlated rows per episode, early stopping leaked and never fired honestly. Added an episode-wise split, weight decay, a cosine loss, a smaller network.

    per-step error −18%, reach 9 → 6 no effect

  6. Rollout loss, short horizon

    Train against the clone's own accumulated target instead of scoring each step in isolation, unrolling 16 steps.

    drift unchanged no effect

    At 16 steps the drift is only about 0.35 rad. The loss never saw the regime it was built to fix.

  7. Rollout loss, long horizon

    The same loss unrolled to 120 steps, with the rollout term weighted five times the one-step term.

    drift 3.2 → 0.19 rad, a 16× reduction that stops growing — reach 5/78 mechanism fixed, outcome unchanged

Every fix landed. None of them mattered.

We fixed every mechanism we could measure. What is left is the one thing behavioral cloning structurally cannot touch.

The drift measurement is teacher-forced: states come from the expert's own trajectory. So the long-rollout clone tracks the expert's commands almost perfectly along a path the expert chose. In deployment the clone chooses the path, and correct commands on the wrong trajectory are worth nothing. Correcting that requires the simulator inside the training loop — which is reinforcement learning, not cloning.

What the training set actually looks like

Recommendation

  • Keep the clone, change its job. Use it as an initialization for reinforcement learning, where being imperfect is permitted. The reacher's warm-start arm took a 79/120 clone to the same return asymptote as training from scratch while leading by 19 scenarios at 100k steps.
  • Run vanilla RL as the control. On both prior systems it matched or beat the residual at convergence, and it tells us whether 70/78 was ever the ceiling.
  • Still untested: closed-loop training with the simulator in the loop. Separately, matching the reacher's relative data density would cost roughly 180 worker-hours of solver time.

Caveats

  • Single seed throughout. Differences of one to four scenarios are noise; the observed band across configurations is 1–9.
  • The effective dimension is 5.1 ± 0.5, with individual fits between 4.2 and 5.8. Because it is an exponent, every cost multiplier here is order-of-magnitude rather than precise.
  • The row-wise validation split made early clone validation numbers optimistic by roughly 10–13×, including the reacher figures reported in journey 13.
